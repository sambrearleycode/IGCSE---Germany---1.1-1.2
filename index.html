Threat Comparator: Left vs. Right (1919–1920)

Cambridge examiners love asking: "Why was the Kapp Putsch a greater danger to the Weimar government than the Spartacist Uprising?" Compare them side-by-side.

January 1919 · Far-Left Revolt

Spartacist Uprising

Led by Karl Liebknecht & Rosa Luxemburg

Communist (KPD)
In Short: Left-wing workers tried to launch a Russian-style communist revolution in Berlin. They had numbers (100,000 strikers), but lacked military weapons and organization.
What did they want?

Get rid of Ebert’s capitalist government; set up a republic run by workers’ and soldiers’ councils (Räte).

How did the military react?

The government sent the regular army and armed volunteer ex-soldiers called the Freikorps. They crushed the communists with ruthless brutality.

The Human Cost & Punishment:

Over 170 workers killed. Liebknecht and Luxemburg were murdered without a trial. Luxemburg’s body was dumped into a canal.

Long-Term Impact on Democracy:

It broke the political Left in two. The Communists could never forgive the SPD for using right-wing militias to kill their leaders. They refused to join together even when Hitler rose to power in 1933.

March 1920 · Far-Right Revolt

Kapp Putsch

Led by Wolfgang Kapp & General von Lüttwitz

Nationalist / Military
In Short: Right-wing soldiers marched into Berlin to overthrow the republic. The regular German army refused to stop them. The government was saved only by a massive peaceful workers' strike.
What did they want?

Stop the government from reducing the army to 100,000 men (required by the Treaty of Versailles), restore imperial power, and cancel democracy.

How did the military react?

Army Commander General von Seeckt refused Ebert’s orders: "Reichswehr does not fire upon Reichswehr." The army deserted its own government.

The Human Cost & Punishment:

Kapp fled to Sweden after five days. The leaders received virtually no punishment from sympathetic conservative judges.

Long-Term Impact on Democracy:

It proved that the army was a "state within a state." The Weimar government could not count on its own generals to defend it against right-wing traitors.

Key Murder: Walther Rathenau (June 1922)

Foreign Minister Walther Rathenau was gunned down in Berlin by nationalist extremists. He was targeted for three key reasons: he was Jewish, wealthy, and pursued Erfüllungspolitik (trying to follow the Versailles treaty to prove its terms were impossible). Over one million Germans marched in his funeral procession.

Exam Analysis Prompt (AO2)

Why did Scheidemann proclaim the Republic so urgently without consulting Chancellor Friedrich Ebert?

Scheidemann knew Karl Liebknecht of the Spartacists was about to proclaim a "Free Socialist Republic" from the Berlin Palace. Ebert was furious because he wanted a constitutional national assembly to decide the form of state, but Scheidemann acted unilaterally to prevent a communist revolution.

Model Evaluative Conclusion Framework (Level 5)

Cambridge 6-Mark Causation Explainer

"Why was the Kapp Putsch defeated in March 1920?"

To get full 6 marks, provide two fully explained causal factors with links to the outcome (PEEL structure).

1 Factor 1: The General Strike of the Working Class

When Ebert’s government fled to Stuttgart, they asked all workers to strike. Berlin completely froze: no water, no gas, no trains, and no electricity. Because civil servants and bank clerks also refused to work, Wolfgang Kapp could not run the government or pay his soldiers.

2 Factor 2: Lack of Support from the Civil Service

Even though the regular army did not fight Kapp, General von Seeckt also refused to actively join him. The civil servants in Berlin quietly refused to follow Kapp's orders, leaving him completely isolated after just five days.

Cambridge 4-Mark Knowledge Recall

"Describe the events of the Spartacist Uprising in January 1919."

A 4-mark question requires 4 clear, separate factual statements.

Point 1: In January 1919, the Spartacist League (led by Karl Liebknecht and Rosa Luxemburg) staged an armed uprising in Berlin.
Point 2: They seized newspaper offices and organized a strike of over 100,000 workers to establish a communist government.
Point 3: Defense Minister Gustav Noske mobilized the Freikorps (anti-communist ex-soldiers) to crush the rebellion with heavy weapons.
Point 4: Over 170 people were killed in street fighting, and both Liebknecht and Luxemburg were murdered without trial.

Cambridge Senior Examiner Mark Scheme (0470/0977)

How examiners grade 4-mark, 6-mark, and 10-mark questions

Assessment Objective (AO) Breakdown:

AO1 (Knowledge & Recall): Accurate dates, pact names, actors, and specific numbers.

AO2 (Historical Analysis & Causation): Explaining reasons, weighing factors, comparing significance, and forming balanced conclusions.

Levels of Response for 10-Mark Questions:
Level 5 (9–10 marks): Balanced argument explaining BOTH sides (doomed vs. resilient), culminating in a sustained, substantiated judgment that reaches a clear conclusion.
Level 4 (7–8 marks): Explains BOTH sides with specific factual evidence, but conclusion is descriptive or asserted without deep evaluation.
Level 3 (4–6 marks): Explains ONE side well (e.g. only argues Weimar was doomed), or provides narrative with some explanation.
Level 2 (2–3 marks): Identifies factual points without explanatory links to the question.
Senior Examiner Secret: To score in Level 5, do NOT merely write "It was a bit of both." Distinguish between short-term survival (the republic survived 1918–1920 through working-class strikes and pacts) and long-term structural weakness (the reliance on unpunished reactionary elites sowed the seeds for collapse in 1930–1933).
