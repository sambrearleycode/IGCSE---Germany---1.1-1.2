Unit 1.3 · Constitutional Engineering & Fatal Flaws · Signed 11 August 1919

The Weimar Constitution: Strengths & Fatal Flaws

Pitted as the most democratic constitution in the world, Weimar gave all men and women the vote. Yet, its mathematical election system (Proportional Representation) and emergency loophole (Article 48) created chronic instability.

In a Nutshell (The 3 Core Ideas)
1. Great Democratic Freedoms: All men and women over 20 could vote. A Bill of Rights protected free speech, religious liberty, and a free press.
2. PR Weakened Parliament: Parties got seats matching their exact % of votes. This caused small extremist parties to flourish and led to 25 weak coalition governments.
3. Article 48 Loophole: The President could rule by emergency decree without the Reichstag. Ebert used it 136 times; later it became a tool for legal dictatorship.

Why Did Proportional Representation Paralyze Weimar?

Under PR, a party getting 2% of the vote won 2% of Reichstag seats. See why no party ever reached a 50% majority:

Seat Distribution (Total 459 Seats): 50% Majority Threshold = 230 Seats
Coalition Result: 3-Party Compromise (SPD + Center + DDP)

Because no party gained over 50% of the seats, moderate parties had to form fragile multi-party coalitions. When these parties argued over welfare or taxes, the government collapsed. Over 14 years, Weimar had 25 separate governments, causing ordinary Germans to lose faith in democracy.

Unit 1.4 · The 1923 Year of Crisis (Part 1) · January 1923 Invasion

The Ruhr Occupation & Passive Resistance

When Germany defaulted on timber and coal reparations, French and Belgian troops invaded the Ruhr industrial valley. German Chancellor Wilhelm Cuno ordered a regional general strike called Passive Resistance.

The Domino Chain of the 1923 Crisis

Step 01: Default

Reparations Default

Germany fell behind on delivering coal and telephone poles to France. The total reparations bill was £6.6 billion (132 billion gold marks).

Step 02: Invasion

French Troops Enter

French PM Raymond Poincaré sent 60,000 troops to occupy the Ruhr to seize coal and steel directly from German factories as payment in kind.

Step 03: Strike

Passive Resistance

German workers refused to mine coal or run trains. French soldiers shot dead 130 strikers and expelled 100,000 protesters.

Step 04: Collapse

Printing Presses Roll

With no tax income from the Ruhr, the government printed paper money to pay the striking workers' wages, triggering catastrophic hyperinflation.

The Economic Disaster of Passive Resistance: Financial Hemorrhage
Tax Revenue Lost: The Ruhr produced 80% of Germany’s coal and steel. Tax revenue stopped completely.
Paying 2 Million Strikers: The government promised to pay full wages to workers on strike despite having zero income.
Importing Foreign Coal: Germany had to use scarce foreign currency to buy coal from Britain to keep lights on.
Unit 1.4 · The 1923 Year of Crisis (Part 2) · Currency Collapse & Social Trauma

The Hyperinflation Crisis & The Social Fallout

When paper money was printed without real economic goods backing it, the German Mark lost all value. Prices rose by the hour, wiping out the middle class and creating deep social trauma.

What Did Hyperinflation Look Like in Daily Life?
Wheelbarrows for Shopping: People carried cash in laundry baskets and wheelbarrows just to buy butter and milk.
Paid Twice Daily: Workers sprinted to shops during lunch because prices doubled by dinnertime.
Return to Barter: Shopkeepers refused paper money; people traded coal for sausages or shoes for eggs.
Fuel & Wallpaper: Banknotes were burned in stoves for heat because burning cash was cheaper than buying wood.

Winners vs. Losers of Hyperinflation

Hyperinflation did not hurt everyone equally. Examine who gained and who was ruined:

THE WINNERS Profited from Crisis
  • Industrialists & Big Business (e.g. Hugo Stinnes): Borrowed millions from banks and repaid loans with worthless marks; bought out bankrupt competitors for pennies.
  • Mortgage Holders & Debtors: Homeowners wiped out 30-year house mortgages with what amounted to the price of a box of matches.
  • Foreign Tourists & Diplomats: Exchanged a few US dollars or British pounds for billions of marks and lived in luxury hotels.
THE LOSERS Devastated
  • The Middle Class (Mittelstand): Careful savers, teachers, doctors, and small shopkeepers lost their entire life savings and insurance policies overnight.
  • Pensioners & War Widows: Fixed monthly pensions could not buy a cup of tea. Thousands faced starvation and absolute poverty.
  • Ordinary Wage Earners: Although wages were raised, they could not keep pace with hourly price hikes.
Senior Examiner Significance (AO2): The real tragedy was psychological. The hardworking middle class felt that playing by the rules was a sucker's game and that the democratic republic had cheated them. When the Great Depression hit in 1929, these scarred families refused to support the republic and turned to Adolf Hitler.
Unit 1.4 · The 1923 Year of Crisis (Part 3) · 8–9 November 1923

The Munich Putsch: Tactical Fiasco to Propaganda Triumph

When Gustav Stresemann called off passive resistance, Adolf Hitler tried to seize power in Munich. Although the march on Munich was smashed by police, Hitler turned the trial into an enormous national victory.

Events of the Putsch: Step-by-Step

8 Nov 1923 · 8:30 PM

Bürgerbräukeller Beer Hall

Hitler and 600 armed SA storm a political meeting. Hitler fires a pistol into the ceiling and forces Bavarian leaders (Kahr, Lossow, Seisser) at gunpoint to back his revolution.

Overnight · 8–9 Nov

Bavarian Leaders Renounce

Once allowed to leave the beer hall, Kahr and Lossow immediately alert the Munich police and military. Hitler’s surprise coup is compromised.

9 Nov 1923 · Midday

The Feldherrnhalle Clash

Hitler and Ludendorff lead 2,000 Nazis into central Munich. Armed police open fire. 16 Nazis and 4 police die. Hitler dislocates his shoulder, flees in a yellow car, and is arrested 2 days later.

Why the Munich Putsch Became an Ultimate Success for Hitler:

1. National Trial Platform: His trial for treason was reported on the front page of every newspaper. His fiery speeches made him famous across all of Germany.
2. Lenient Imperial Judge: The right-wing judge gave him the minimum sentence (5 years) and let him out after only 9 months of comfortable custody in Landsberg.
3. Wrote Mein Kampf: In prison, Hitler dictated his political manifesto to Rudolf Hess, outlining his plans to conquer Europe and eliminate Jews.
4. Shift in Strategy: Hitler realized armed coups failed. He committed the Nazis to entering democratic elections to "destroy democracy from within."

Cambridge 6-Mark Causation Explainer

"Why did hyperinflation occur in Germany in 1923?"

To score 6/6 marks, provide two fully linked causal factors:

1 Factor 1: The French Invasion of the Ruhr

When Germany defaulted on coal reparations, French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr. The German government ordered passive resistance, halting coal and industrial production. Because the Ruhr generated 80% of German tax revenue, the state suffered a near-total loss of income.

2 Factor 2: Printing Unbacked Paper Currency

To pay the wages of 2 million striking workers and cover national debts, the government printed massive volumes of paper banknotes. Because no real economic output was backing this newly printed money, the value of the mark collapsed completely, causing hourly price hyperinflation.

Cambridge 4-Mark Recall Explainer

"Describe the events of the Munich Putsch in November 1923."

Point 1: On 8 November 1923, Adolf Hitler and 600 SA men stormed the Bürgerbräukeller beer hall in Munich.
Point 2: At gunpoint, Hitler forced Bavarian state leaders (including Gustav von Kahr) to support his national revolution.
Point 3: On 9 November, Hitler and General Ludendorff led 2,000 Nazis marching toward central Munich, but were met by armed police.
Point 4: In a shootout at the Feldherrnhalle, 16 Nazis and 4 police officers were killed, the putsch collapsed, and Hitler was arrested.
